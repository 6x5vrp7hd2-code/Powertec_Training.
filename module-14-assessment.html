Module 14 Assessment

Power Quality — Harmonics and Other Common Problems

Five multiple-choice questions followed by five written questions. Answers are hidden until you continue to the review page.

Part A — Multiple Choice

QUESTION 1

What are THDi and THDu?

QUESTION 2

Why do triplen harmonics (3rd, 9th, 15th…) add up in the neutral conductor instead of cancelling?

QUESTION 3

According to IEEE 519, why are voltage THD limits generally stricter (lower) at higher system voltages?

QUESTION 4

Why does an average-responding (non-TRMS) clamp meter give an unreliable reading on a harmonic-rich data centre circuit?

QUESTION 5

What is the recommended remedy for resonance risk when installing a tuned passive harmonic filter or PFC capacitor bank?

Part B — Written Understanding

WRITTEN QUESTION 1

Explain the mechanism by which non-linear loads (UPS rectifiers, VFDs, switch-mode power supplies) generate harmonic currents, and how those currents become voltage harmonics that affect other, unrelated loads.

WRITTEN QUESTION 2

Explain why a generator supply produces a much higher THDu for the same harmonic- rich load compared to a utility supply, and what practical risks this creates.

WRITTEN QUESTION 3

Describe the IEEE 519 philosophy of “shared responsibility” between utility and end-user for harmonic control, and explain how the current-distortion limit scaling by Isc/IL ratio supports this philosophy.

WRITTEN QUESTION 4

Explain at least three of the “other classic problems” listed in the guide’s section 14.4 table (e.g., voltage sags, phase unbalance, circulating currents in the PE), including their typical cause and remedy.

WRITTEN QUESTION 5

Why does the guide warn that “a power quality measurement made on a quiet Tuesday says nothing about behaviour at the worst moment”? Describe what a proper measurement campaign should cover instead.
