Module 17 Assessment

Interlocks — Read a Diagram and Never Be Caught Out by Them

Five multiple-choice questions followed by five written questions. Answers are hidden until you continue to the review page.

Part A — Multiple Choice

QUESTION 1

What is the fundamental principle of a trapped-key interlock system?

QUESTION 2

In the “2 keys for 3 breakers” busbar tie interlock, why can the two incomers never be paralleled through the tie?

QUESTION 3

What does “VPIS off = indication only” mean in relation to an interlock?

QUESTION 4

What four things must be documented whenever an interlock is deliberately defeated (neutralised)?

QUESTION 5

What is Zone Selective Interlocking (ZSI)?

Part B — Written Understanding

WRITTEN QUESTION 1

Explain why a trapped-key interlock is described as providing “physical proof,” and contrast this with a purely procedural or administrative safety rule that relies on someone remembering a step.

WRITTEN QUESTION 2

Using the earthing switch example, describe step by step how the trapped-key mechanism makes it physically impossible to close an earthing switch onto a live circuit, and impossible to re-energise a circuit while its earth is closed.

WRITTEN QUESTION 3

Explain the three families of interlock (mechanical trapped key, electromechanical, logic/PLC) and give one data centre example of each.

WRITTEN QUESTION 4

Describe why “an interlock that resists is an interlock talking to you,” and explain the correct response when a manoeuvre unexpectedly resists during a MOP.

WRITTEN QUESTION 5

Explain the purpose of the key register and key exchange diagram, and describe what an “active familiarisation” walkdown involves when arriving at an unfamiliar site with dozens of keys.
