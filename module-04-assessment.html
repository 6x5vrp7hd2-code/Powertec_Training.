Module 04 Assessment

Basic Protections

Five multiple-choice questions followed by five written questions. Answers are hidden until you continue to the review page.

Part A — Multiple Choice

QUESTION 1

What does an HRC fuse do when current exceeds its rated value?

QUESTION 2

What are the two release types normally combined inside a circuit breaker?

QUESTION 3

What principle underlies the operation of a residual current device (RCD)?

QUESTION 4

In the TT earthing system, why is an RCD not optional but mandatory?

QUESTION 5

What does an insulation monitoring device (IMD) do in an IT earthing system?

Part B — Written Understanding

WRITTEN QUESTION 1

Explain why a fuse “is replaced, not reset” and describe the operational consequence of this for a data centre maintenance team.

WRITTEN QUESTION 2

Describe the difference between an RCD’s detection principle and a standard circuit breaker’s detection principle, and explain why this makes the RCD uniquely suited to detecting small earth faults.

WRITTEN QUESTION 3

Explain why the IMD does not trip the circuit on a first fault in an IT system, and what organisational requirement this creates for the site.

WRITTEN QUESTION 4

A PDU feeds 40 servers, each with a few milliamps of normal EMC filter leakage current. Explain why this can cause nuisance tripping of a 30 mA RCD, and why this is a key reason TT is avoided in critical IT distribution.

WRITTEN QUESTION 5

Compare the fuse, circuit breaker, RCD and IMD in terms of what fault type each is principally designed to catch.
