Module 19 Assessment

The MOP — Complete Drafting Methodology

Five multiple-choice questions followed by five written questions. Answers are hidden until you continue to the review page.

Part A — Multiple Choice

QUESTION 1

What is the key difference between a MOP, an SOP, and an EOP?

QUESTION 2

What is a “hold point” in a MOP?

QUESTION 3

Why must each MOP action line contain only one imperative verb (“Open QF12,” never “open QF12 and check the load then close QF14”)?

QUESTION 4

Why does the guide state “if the reviewer asks a question, the MOP is incomplete”?

QUESTION 5

What are the six stages of the methodology for building a MOP sequence “without getting lost,” as described by the guide?

Part B — Written Understanding

WRITTEN QUESTION 1

Explain the nine-part standard structure of a MOP as described by the guide, and describe why the “back-out plan” section is considered essential even for a well- planned operation.

WRITTEN QUESTION 2

Describe the six-stage methodology for building a MOP sequence, and explain why “let the interlocks dictate the order” is placed before “validation walkdown” rather than after.

WRITTEN QUESTION 3

Explain why the guide insists on imperative, single-action lines with an observable expected result for every MOP step, using an example of what NOT to write versus what TO write.

WRITTEN QUESTION 4

Describe the purpose of a “table-top / dry run” review, and explain what kind of errors this step is specifically designed to catch that a solo read-through would not.

WRITTEN QUESTION 5

Explain why the guide states that “the stop rule” (any deviation between expected and observed result = STOP) belongs to every participant, not just the work leader, and describe the practical consequence of this principle during execution.
