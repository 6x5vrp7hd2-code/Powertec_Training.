The MV Substation and Its Switchgear

QUESTION 1

What is a Ring Main Unit (RMU)?

YOUR ANSWER

No answer recorded.

CORRECT ANSWER

A) A compact, sealed MV switchgear type widely used for distribution due to its small footprint

QUESTION 2

What is the purpose of the VPIS (Voltage Presence Indicating System)?

YOUR ANSWER

No answer recorded.

CORRECT ANSWER

A) To indicate, via lamps fed by capacitive dividers, whether a cubicle phase is live — as an indication only, not a proof

QUESTION 3

What does DGPT2 (or the older Buchholz relay) detect on an oil-immersed transformer?

YOUR ANSWER

No answer recorded.

CORRECT ANSWER

A) Gas, pressure, and temperature changes indicating an internal fault, sending a trip signal to the associated cubicle

QUESTION 4

What is the key difference between a DM1 (circuit breaker) and QM/PM (switch-fuse combination) transformer protection cubicle?

YOUR ANSWER

No answer recorded.

CORRECT ANSWER

A) DM1 uses an adjustable digital protection relay; QM/PM relies solely on the fixed rating of the HRC fuses, with no adjustable relay

QUESTION 5

Why does the metering cubicle (CM/GBC) have no switching device?

YOUR ANSWER

No answer recorded.

CORRECT ANSWER

A) Its function is purely metrological measurement (VT/CT for the utility meter), and its covers carry utility seals that must never be touched without the utility present

WRITTEN QUESTION 1

Explain why the VPIS is described as “an indication, never a proof,” and describe what must always be done in addition before working on a cubicle.

YOUR RESPONSE

No response recorded.

MODEL ANSWER / KEY POINTS

The VPIS lamps are fed by a tiny capacitively-coupled signal picked off the bushings — lit means the phase is very likely live, but “off” could equally mean a blown lamp or a failed capacitive divider, not necessarily an absent voltage. Because of this failure mode, the VPIS can never be relied on as proof of a dead circuit; the mandatory step in addition is a full voltage absence test using a compliant, independently tested detector (tested on a known live source before and after the measurement).

WRITTEN QUESTION 2

Compare the DM1 (circuit breaker) and QM/PM (switch-fuse) transformer protection cubicle variants, including when each is typically used and how each is identified on a diagram and in the field.

YOUR RESPONSE

No response recorded.

MODEL ANSWER / KEY POINTS

DM1 uses a true MV circuit breaker (SF6 or vacuum) with CTs feeding a digital protection relay offering adjustable, remotely signalled protection functions (ANSI 50/51 overcurrent, 50N/51N earth fault, DGPT2/Buchholz trip input) — the standard choice above roughly 800–1,250 kVA or wherever adjustable, remotely visible protection is wanted. QM/PM instead uses a switch-disconnector combined with three HRC fuses fitted with a striker (so one blown fuse trips all three phases); there is no adjustable relay, since the protection level is fixed entirely by the fuse rating — a simpler, lower- cost solution for modest transformer ratings. On the diagram, DM1 shows a breaker cross plus CT and relay symbols; QM/PM shows a switch plus three fuse rectangles in series.

WRITTEN QUESTION 3

Describe the function and significance of the bus tie cubicle between two half-busbars, and explain the “2 keys for 3 breakers” interlock referenced in relation to it.

YOUR RESPONSE

No response recorded.

MODEL ANSWER / KEY POINTS

The bus tie cubicle joins or splits two half-busbars fed by separate incomers, allowing the whole substation to be fed from a single incomer during maintenance of the other. The “2 keys for 3 breakers” interlock exists because only two keys circulate among the three locks (incomer A, incomer B, tie): since closing any breaker consumes and traps a key, at most two of the three breakers can ever be closed simultaneously — meaning the two incomers can never be paralleled through the tie, protecting against paralleling two non-synchronised MV sources and against exceeding the switchgear’s combined breaking capacity.

WRITTEN QUESTION 4

Explain why the metering cubicle is treated differently from every other cubicle in the substation in terms of who may work on it.

YOUR RESPONSE

No response recorded.

MODEL ANSWER / KEY POINTS

The metering cubicle houses metrological measurement equipment (VTs, metering CTs) whose accuracy and integrity are governed by contractual/regulatory billing requirements between the utility and the customer. Its covers carry the utility’s own physical seals, and tampering with a sealed metering circuit is both a technical risk (compromising billing accuracy) and typically a contractual/legal breach — hence the guide’s instruction to never work on it without the utility present.

WRITTEN QUESTION 5

Using the cubicle summary table’s logic, explain how a specialist would identify, purely from visual inspection in the field (without a diagram), which type of cubicle they are looking at.

YOUR RESPONSE

No response recorded.

MODEL ANSWER / KEY POINTS

Incomer (IM): switch-disconnector, downstream earthing switch, VPIS lamps, cables at the bottom, no fuses visible. Transformer protection (QM/PM): a fuse-cover door or window on the front showing rating labels, blown-fuse indicators. Transformer protection (DM1): a digital protection relay screen, trip LED, spring-charging handle. Metering (CM/GBC): utility seals visible on covers, no operable switching device at all. Bus tie + riser: positioned in the middle of the cubicle line-up, showing a through-bar mimic and a distinctive multi-key interlock arrangement.
