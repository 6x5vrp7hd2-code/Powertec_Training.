Module 05 Assessment

Earthing Systems — What Happens to the Fault Current?

Five multiple-choice questions followed by five written questions. Answers are hidden until you continue to the review page.

Part A — Multiple Choice

QUESTION 1

In the IEC 60364 two-letter code, what does the first letter describe?

QUESTION 2

Why is TN-S considered the standard for critical data centre distribution?

QUESTION 3

What is the defining risk of the TN-C system when used downstream of a UPS?

QUESTION 4

What happens at the first insulation fault in a well-designed IT earthing system?

QUESTION 5

What is “PME” (Protective Multiple Earthing), as commonly used in UK/Irish public LV distribution?

Part B — Written Understanding

WRITTEN QUESTION 1

Explain, in sequence, what happens to the fault current in each of the four earthing systems (TT, TN-S, TN-C, IT) at the moment of a first insulation fault.

WRITTEN QUESTION 2

Why must an installation never be converted from TN-S back to TN-C (“only in the direction C to S”)? Explain the physical reasoning.

WRITTEN QUESTION 3

Describe why a data centre typically contains several different earthing systems simultaneously, giving at least three examples of where different systems might be used within the same site.

WRITTEN QUESTION 4

Explain the “neutral during source transfers” problem: why does a 3-pole (unswitched neutral) transfer between two earthed sources create circulating currents and nuisance ground-fault tripping in a TN-S system, and how does a 4-pole transfer solve this?

WRITTEN QUESTION 5

Compare the relative safety and practicality trade-offs of TT, TN-S, TN-C, and IT for a critical data centre load, referencing at least one advantage and one drawback of each.
