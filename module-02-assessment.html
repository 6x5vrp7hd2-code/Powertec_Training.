Module 02 Assessment

Conductors — Phase, Neutral, PE and Exposed Conductive Parts

Five multiple-choice questions followed by five written questions. Answers are hidden until you continue to the review page.

Part A — Multiple Choice

QUESTION 1

What is the standard IEC colour for the protective earth (PE) conductor?

QUESTION 2

Why is the neutral conductor considered a live, dangerous conductor even though it is a “return” path?

QUESTION 3

What does a PEN conductor combine?

QUESTION 4

In normal operation (no fault present), how much current should flow in the PE conductor?

QUESTION 5

What is an “exposed conductive part” (masse)?

Part B — Written Understanding

WRITTEN QUESTION 1

Explain the two distinct reasons for connecting parts of an electrical installation to earth, and why they must not be confused with each other.

WRITTEN QUESTION 2

Describe what happens, step by step, if a phase conductor touches an exposed conductive part that IS properly bonded to the PE, versus one that is NOT bonded.

WRITTEN QUESTION 3

Explain why a PEN conductor is acceptable upstream of a data centre’s transformer but forbidden downstream of the UPS.

WRITTEN QUESTION 4

Using the touch-voltage figures given (human body resistance ~1,000–2,000 Ω, 230 V supply), explain how the 30 mA and 10 mA thresholds relate to real physiological danger.

WRITTEN QUESTION 5

Why does the guide say the neutral “can carry more current than the phases” is possible, and under what circumstance does this happen (cross-reference with the concept developed further in section 14)?
