Module 09 Assessment

Switching Devices and Their Performance

Five multiple-choice questions followed by five written questions. Answers are hidden until you continue to the review page.

Part A — Multiple Choice

QUESTION 1

What can a disconnector (isolator) NOT do?

QUESTION 2

What does Icu (ultimate breaking capacity) represent?

QUESTION 3

Why must HRC fuses always be replaced in sets of three, even if only one has blown?

QUESTION 4

What is the single most dangerous mistake associated with draw-out circuit breakers?

QUESTION 5

What is the defining feature of a switch-disconnector (load break switch)?

Part B — Written Understanding

WRITTEN QUESTION 1

Explain the difference between a disconnector, a switch-disconnector, and a circuit breaker in terms of what each device can safely interrupt, and why confusing them is described as a historical cause of serious accidents.

WRITTEN QUESTION 2

Describe the difference between Icu and Ics, and explain why a coupling operation between two half-busbars requires a study of combined short-circuit currents against these values.

WRITTEN QUESTION 3

Explain the purpose of the striker pin on an MV switch-fuse combination, and why it matters that it trips all three phases rather than just the phase where the fuse blew.

WRITTEN QUESTION 4

Describe the three positions of a draw-out circuit breaker (connected, test, disconnected) and explain which one is required for isolation and why.

WRITTEN QUESTION 5

Explain the LSIG protection functions found in a modern electronic trip unit, using one example setting for each function.
