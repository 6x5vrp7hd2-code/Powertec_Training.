Module 06 Answer Review

Stored Energy — Capacitors and Batteries

Compare your responses with the supplied answer key and model points.

QUESTION 1

What is the formula for energy stored in a capacitor?

YOUR ANSWER

No answer recorded.

CORRECT ANSWER

A) E = ½ × C × U²

QUESTION 2

Why is a capacitor’s discharge time deliberately slow (several minutes)?

YOUR ANSWER

No answer recorded.

CORRECT ANSWER

A) A bleeder resistor small enough to discharge it quickly would overheat during normal, continuous operation

QUESTION 3

Why does a battery remain dangerous even after all AC sources to a UPS are opened and locked?

YOUR ANSWER

No answer recorded.

CORRECT ANSWER

A) A battery is itself a source that produces voltage by chemical reaction, independent of any AC connection

QUESTION 4

What is the only way to fully isolate a UPS battery?

YOUR ANSWER

No answer recorded.

CORRECT ANSWER

A) Open its own dedicated switching device (QF-BAT), then lock it

QUESTION 5

What additional hazard do lithium-ion batteries introduce compared to VRLA batteries?

YOUR ANSWER

No answer recorded.

CORRECT ANSWER

A) Thermal runaway — a self-sustaining, escalating chemical reaction that cannot simply be stopped by removing electrical power

WRITTEN QUESTION 1

Explain why the manufacturer’s discharge time is described as “a necessary condition, not a proof,” and describe the correct verification procedure that follows it.

YOUR RESPONSE

No response recorded.

MODEL ANSWER / KEY POINTS

The discharge time is based on a typical/nominal bleeder-resistor decay curve, but component tolerances, a failed bleeder resistor, or an atypical capacitor bank could mean the actual voltage after the stated time is still above the safe threshold. Waiting the specified time is necessary before even attempting a measurement, but only a direct voltmeter reading (CAT III 1,000 V DC minimum, on the + and − terminals) confirming less than 50 V DC constitutes proof that the bus is actually safe to work on.

WRITTEN QUESTION 2

Using the formula E = ½CU², explain why doubling the DC bus voltage has a much larger effect on stored energy than doubling the capacitance, and why this matters for UPS safety.

YOUR RESPONSE

No response recorded.

MODEL ANSWER / KEY POINTS

Since energy scales with U², doubling voltage quadruples stored energy, while doubling capacitance only doubles it. A UPS with a 400–800 V DC bus and tens of millifarads of capacitance can therefore store several kilojoules — enough energy to produce a lethal arc — which is why bus voltage, not just capacitance, is the dominant factor engineers must respect when assessing residual capacitor hazard.

WRITTEN QUESTION 3

Describe, step by step, why “opening all the AC inputs of a UPS” does not make the battery circuit safe, and what must be done in addition.

YOUR RESPONSE

No response recorded.

MODEL ANSWER / KEY POINTS

AC isolation only removes the alternating-current sources (rectifier input, bypass input); a battery is a chemical source that produces DC voltage continuously and is entirely independent of the AC circuit — nothing about opening AC breakers touches the battery’s own voltage production. The only way to render the battery circuit safe is to open and lock its own dedicated switching device (QF-BAT), and even then, the battery cabinet side of that device remains live (a battery cannot be isolated from itself, only disconnected from downstream equipment).

WRITTEN QUESTION 4

Explain why shorting a charged capacitor with an uninsulated tool is dangerous, and what the correct alternative is if a discharge tool is needed.

YOUR RESPONSE

No response recorded.

MODEL ANSWER / KEY POINTS

A capacitor’s stored energy, if shorted suddenly with an uninsulated tool, can release as an intense arc that welds the tool to the terminals and throws molten metal — a serious injury risk. The correct approach, if a manual discharge is genuinely needed beyond the passive bleeder resistors, is to use an insulated discharge resistor tool specifically provided by the manufacturer, which limits discharge current to a safe rate rather than creating a near-short-circuit.

WRITTEN QUESTION 5

Compare the specific risks (voltage, arc behaviour, failure mode) of a UPS’s capacitor bus versus its battery string.

YOUR RESPONSE

No response recorded.

MODEL ANSWER / KEY POINTS

The capacitor bus stores a large but finite, passively-decaying amount of energy (kilojoules, discharging over minutes via bleeder resistors) at high DC voltage (400–800 V), with the main risk being a lethal arc if shorted before discharge. The battery string is an active, continuously-replenishing chemical source with enormous short-circuit current capability (internal resistance of a few milliohms can yield on the order of 100,000 A into a bolted short), never “runs down” on its own, and — for lithium chemistries — carries the additional risk of thermal runaway that cannot be halted by electrical isolation alone.
