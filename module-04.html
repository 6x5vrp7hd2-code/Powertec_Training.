Basic Protections

Section 4 from the expanded Data Centre Electrical Specialist’s Guide.

The four protective devices covered (fuse, circuit breaker, RCD, IMD) form a complementary toolkit rather than a hierarchy — each catches a fault type the others cannot.

Additional context:

RCD sensitivity classes and international variation

Beyond the IΔn threshold values mentioned (30 mA, 300 mA, 1 A), international standards define RCD “types” by the waveform they can detect: Type AC (pure sinusoidal residual current only), Type A (also detects pulsating DC residual current, now mandatory in many jurisdictions for circuits feeding electronic equipment), and Type B (also detects smooth DC residual current, required for many EV chargers and some UPS/inverter applications). In data centres, where output power increasingly passes through inverters and rectifiers capable of injecting DC or high-frequency residual components, RCD type selection matters as much as the trip threshold.

GFCI vs RCD terminology

North American practice draws a sharper distinction than European practice between a GFCI (Ground Fault Circuit Interrupter, typically a low- threshold ~5 mA device for personnel protection on receptacles) and a GFPE (Ground Fault Protection of Equipment, a higher-threshold device, e.g. 30 mA–1200 A range, used to protect equipment and limit fire risk rather than protect a person directly) — a distinction the guide’s brief mention of “300 mA or 1 A for fire or installation protection” is gesturing toward.

The insulation monitoring device (IMD) and IT systems

IMDs are mandated by IEC 61557-8 for IT systems, and are widely used not just in continuous-process industries and operating theatres (as the guide notes) but increasingly in renewable energy and battery-storage installations, where an ungrounded/high-impedance DC bus benefits from the same “alarm rather than trip” philosophy — continuity of a solar array or battery string matters more than instant disconnection on a first, low-current fault.
