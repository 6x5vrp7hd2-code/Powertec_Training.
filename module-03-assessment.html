Module 03 Assessment

Faults — Overload, Short-Circuit, Earth Fault

Five multiple-choice questions followed by five written questions. Answers are hidden until you continue to the review page.

Part A — Multiple Choice

QUESTION 1

What current range typically characterises an overload fault?

QUESTION 2

What is “prospective short-circuit current” (Isc)?

QUESTION 3

Why is an earth fault described as “the most insidious” type of fault?

QUESTION 4

What is “incident energy” as used in arc flash assessment?

QUESTION 5

Which protective device characteristic is designed to mirror the real heating behaviour of a cable during an overload?

Part B — Written Understanding

WRITTEN QUESTION 1

Explain why overload and short-circuit faults require fundamentally different types of protective response (timescale and mechanism), and how a modern circuit breaker’s trip unit provides both in one device.

WRITTEN QUESTION 2

Describe the physical effects of an arc flash and explain why incident energy calculations matter for selecting PPE.

WRITTEN QUESTION 3

Explain how the “return path” of an earth fault current depends on the earthing system in use, and what this means for whether an ordinary breaker or an RCD is needed.

WRITTEN QUESTION 4

What is a “short-circuit study” and why must it be performed before installing switchgear at any point in a data centre?

WRITTEN QUESTION 5

Compare the danger profile (speed of onset, typical magnitude, typical consequence) of overload, short-circuit, and earth fault as described in the guide.
