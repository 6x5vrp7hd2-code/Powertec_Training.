The MOP — Complete Drafting Methodology

Section 19 from the expanded Data Centre Electrical Specialist’s Guide.

The Method of Procedure (MOP) — and its close relatives, the Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) and Emergency Operating Procedure (EOP) — form the backbone of planned-work governance across the entire data centre industry, and the specific writing discipline the guide describes (one action, one expected result, per line; explicit stop criteria; independent peer review) reflects widely adopted best practice across critical- infrastructure operations generally, not just electrical work.

Additional context:

MOP/SOP/EOP as an industry-standard document trio

This three-document structure — MOP for planned, non-recurring operations; SOP for recurring, standardised operations; EOP for emergency response — is a naming convention used broadly across data centre operations globally (not exclusive to any one country’s regulatory framework), reflecting a shared industry recognition that different categories of operational risk (planned vs. routine vs. emergency) benefit from differently structured documentation.

The “hold point” concept

Mandatory stops requiring cross-check before a consequential manoeuvre — a concept the guide integrates throughout its worked examples (sections 18.5 and 22) — mirrors “hold points” used in quality assurance and construction inspection regimes internationally: a deliberate point in a sequence where work cannot proceed without an independent verification step, specifically placed immediately before an action that would otherwise be difficult or dangerous to reverse.

Peer review as an error-detection mechanism

The guide’s rule that “if the reviewer asks a question, the MOP is incomplete” reflects a well-established principle in high- reliability organisation theory: a document written and reviewed only by its author is far more likely to contain unstated assumptions the author doesn’t realise they’re making, since the author already knows the context in a way a genuinely independent reviewer does not — this is precisely why aviation checklists, surgical safety checklists, and industrial MOPs alike mandate review by someone who did not write the original document.
