Before / During / After — The Operator’s Checklists

WRITTEN QUESTION 1

Explain why the “before, during, after” checklist structure mirrors practices used in aviation and other high-reliability industries, and describe what each phase is specifically designed to catch or prevent.

YOUR RESPONSE

No response recorded.

MODEL ANSWER / KEY POINTS

The three-phase structure exists because different types of error occur at different points in an operation: before-checks catch preparation gaps (wrong or missing documentation, unnoticed pre-existing faults, unqualified participants) before any physical action begins; during-checks catch execution errors in real time (a deviation from an expected result, an unexpected alarm) while there is still an opportunity to safely stop; after-checks catch completion gaps (a forgotten lock, an unrestored redundancy state) before the site is considered fully returned to normal. Aviation and other high-reliability industries structure their checklists identically because the same logical need — catching different error types at the point they’re most detectable — applies regardless of the specific domain.

WRITTEN QUESTION 2

Describe the “announce aloud, confirm” verbal protocol and explain why it is used even though the same information is already written on the MOP.

YOUR RESPONSE

No response recorded.

MODEL ANSWER / KEY POINTS

Even though the action is written on the MOP, verbalising it aloud forces the person performing it to consciously articulate exactly what they are about to do, which interrupts “autopilot” execution where someone might perform a nearby but incorrect action out of habit or misreading. The second participant’s spoken confirmation (“confirmed, QF12”) additionally creates an explicit, real-time checkpoint where a second set of eyes can catch a wrong device or wrong action before it is physically executed, rather than only after the fact during a later line-by-line MOP review.

WRITTEN QUESTION 3

Explain why “any deviation = stop in a safe state + call operations” is treated as an absolute rule with no informal exceptions (“never tinker during a MOP”).

YOUR RESPONSE

No response recorded.

MODEL ANSWER / KEY POINTS

Any deviation between an expected and observed result signals that the specialist’s understanding of the installation’s actual state may be wrong in some way not yet identified — proceeding despite this uncertainty risks compounding an unknown problem with further actions based on a false picture of reality. “Tinkering” (informally improvising a fix rather than stopping) specifically invalidates the risk analysis that was performed for the planned, reviewed sequence, since any improvised action was never assessed by the peer review or hold-point process — this is why the rule is absolute rather than left to individual judgement in the moment.

WRITTEN QUESTION 4

Describe the specific purpose of the counted-removal principle for locks and earths, and explain what failure mode it is specifically designed to catch.

YOUR RESPONSE

No response recorded.

MODEL ANSWER / KEY POINTS

The counted-removal principle catches the single most dangerous and common LOTO- related failure: a lock or earth that was genuinely applied during isolation but is then forgotten and left in place (or, in the more dangerous reverse case, a device believed to be locked/earthed that in fact never received its lock or earth in the first place). By requiring the number removed to exactly equal the number applied — a simple, unambiguous arithmetic check — any discrepancy immediately signals that something is not accounted for, forcing investigation before the equipment is considered safe to re- energise.

WRITTEN QUESTION 5

Explain why “hot lessons learned” are captured immediately after an operation (in the after-checklist) rather than at some later, more convenient time.

YOUR RESPONSE

No response recorded.

MODEL ANSWER / KEY POINTS

Capturing lessons learned immediately, while the details are fresh and the specific deviations, surprises, or near-misses are still clearly remembered by everyone involved, produces far more accurate and useful input for improving the MOP than trying to reconstruct the same details days or weeks later from memory. This immediacy is what allows the guide’s final step (“MOP update for next time”) to genuinely reflect what actually happened during execution, rather than a filtered or incomplete recollection.
