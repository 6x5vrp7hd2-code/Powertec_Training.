Module 07 Assessment

The Data Centre Power Chain — Overview

Five multiple-choice questions followed by five written questions. Answers are hidden until you continue to the review page.

Part A — Multiple Choice

QUESTION 1

What does “concurrent maintainability” mean in data centre design?

QUESTION 2

In an N+1 redundancy topology, what happens when one module is deliberately isolated for maintenance and no other module is already faulty?

QUESTION 3

According to the Uptime Institute Tier classification system, what distinguishes Tier III from Tier II?

QUESTION 4

What must a specialist calculate before isolating any piece of equipment in a redundant data centre?

QUESTION 5

Why is an “N+1 site with one module already faulty” effectively operating at “N”?

Part B — Written Understanding

WRITTEN QUESTION 1

Explain the difference between N, N+1, 2N, and distributed redundant (3N/2) topologies, including the operational consequence of isolating one unit of capacity in each.

WRITTEN QUESTION 2

Why does the guide describe residual load calculation as “reflex no. 1 of the specialist”? Explain what could go wrong if this step were skipped.

WRITTEN QUESTION 3

Compare the Uptime Institute’s Tier III and Tier IV classifications in terms of what each guarantees and the practical cost/complexity trade-off between them.

WRITTEN QUESTION 4

Explain why an N+1 site with one module already in a faulted state effectively becomes an N site, and why this matters before authorising any new isolation.

WRITTEN QUESTION 5

Using the guide’s redundancy topology table, explain what “load transfers are cross- linked” means for distributed redundant topologies, and why the up-to-date load transfer scheme is essential before any operation.
